Dataset

ML Olympiad Autism Prediction

The dataset comes from the Kaggle ML Olympiad - Autism Prediction Challenge, originally sourced from ASD screening data collected across multiple countries.

The dataset contains 800 records with 21 features per record, including 10 AQ-10 behavioural screening scores (A1–A10), demographic variables, and clinical history.

  • 800 records from real ASD screening sessions
  • AQ-10 questions: A1_Score to A10_Score (0 or 1)
  • Demographics: age, gender, ethnicity, country
  • Clinical: jaundice at birth, family ASD history
  • Target: Class/ASD (0 = negative, 1 = positive)
  • 46% ASD positive, 54% ASD negative

AQ-10 Protocol

Official AQ-10 Questions Used in Dataset

The AQ-10 (Autism Spectrum Quotient) is a 10-item screening tool. Each question is scored 1 (ASD indicator) or 0. A score ≥ 6 meets the referral threshold per clinical guidelines.

Methodology

Model Approach (Kaggle Notebook)

🧹

Data Preprocessing

Missing values handled, '?' and 'Others' normalised. Yes/No binary encoded. Age log-transformed to remove positive skew. Random OverSampler used to balance minority class.

🌲

Random Forest Classifier

Best-performing model across 6 classifiers tested. Achieves 98.7% accuracy. Feature importance ranks A7, A10, A1, and result (AQ-10 sum) as top predictors.

📏

AQ-10 Scoring Logic

Sum of A1–A10 answers. Class/ASD=1 when score ≥ 6 (or ≥ 7 in some variants). This threshold corresponds to the clinical referral guideline from NICE/Cambridge ARC.

📊

Feature Selection

Used feature importance from RF and correlation analysis. A1–A10 scores are dominant predictors. Demographic features (age, gender, jaundice, family) provide secondary weight.

⚠️ Important Disclaimer

This platform is built for educational and demonstration purposes, using publicly available Kaggle data. It does not provide, replace, or constitute a formal clinical diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder. The AQ-10 is a screening tool only — a high score indicates that referral for professional assessment may be warranted, but does not mean a person has ASD. All results should be reviewed by a qualified healthcare professional. If you or someone you know has concerns about ASD, please seek a comprehensive evaluation from a licensed clinician.
